Hydrogeological Survey

Before a single shilling goes into the ground, our licensed hydrogeologists run a geophysical resistivity and VES survey to pinpoint the aquifer, the expected depth, and the likely yield — then compile the report you need for your WRA permit.

  • Vertical Electrical Sounding (VES) & resistivity profiling
  • Recommended drilling point, depth & expected yield
  • Full report accepted by WRA & NEMA
  • Turnaround in 3–5 working days

Frequently Asked
Questions

Everything Kenyans ask us before drilling. Still unsure? We're one call away.

Talk to a real engineer 0705 901 445 info@waterwallboreholedrilling.co.ke
How much does it cost to drill a borehole in Kenya?

Cost depends on depth, geology, casing type and how far the site is from our yard. A shallow borehole in soft formation costs far less than a 250m hole through hard basement rock. That's why we quote only after the hydrogeological survey — you get a written, itemised figure covering survey, permit, drilling, casing, test pumping and pump installation, with no surprise charges later. Call 0705 901 445 for an indicative range for your area.

How long does the whole process take?

Typically 4 to 6 weeks end to end. The survey takes 3–5 days, the WRA permit 2–4 weeks (this is the longest wait and it's outside our control), actual drilling 2–4 days, test pumping 1–2 days, and pump plus solar installation another 2–3 days.

Do I need a permit to drill a borehole?

Yes. Kenyan law requires authorisation from the Water Resources Authority (WRA) before drilling, supported by a hydrogeological survey report and NEMA documentation. We prepare and submit the entire application on your behalf — you simply provide your title deed or lease and ID/KRA details.

What if you don't strike water?

Our survey-first approach gives us a 98% strike rate. In the rare case a hole is dry, we sit down with you, review the geophysics, and agree a fair way forward — usually drilling deeper or relocating to the secondary point identified in your survey report, at heavily reduced mobilisation cost. We put this in writing in your contract before work starts.

How deep will my borehole be?

Most boreholes in Kenya fall between 100m and 250m. Coastal and lakeside areas can strike water shallower; parts of Kajiado, Machakos and the central highlands often need 200m+. Your survey report gives a projected depth before we mobilise.

Is the water safe to drink?

We take a sample during test pumping and send it for full physical, chemical and bacteriological analysis. Most Kenyan groundwater is safe, though some areas carry high fluoride, salinity or iron. If treatment is needed we'll recommend and install the right filtration or reverse-osmosis system.

Can solar power run my borehole pump?

Absolutely — and for most clients it's the smartest decision after drilling. We size a solar array to your pump's power and daily volume, so it fills your tank during sunlight hours with zero electricity bill. Hybrid systems that switch to KPLC or a generator on cloudy days are also available.

Do you work outside Nairobi?

Yes — we serve all 47 counties, from Kilifi to Turkana. Mobilisation is quoted transparently based on distance, and for remote sites we simply plan logistics into the schedule.

How much space does the rig need?

We need roughly 10m x 10m of clear working space, plus access wide enough for a truck (about 3.5m) and a water source nearby if possible. Our site supervisor confirms access during the free site visit.

Do you offer payment in stages?

Yes. Payments are structured against milestones — survey, permit, mobilisation and drilling, then equipping. You always know exactly what you're paying for and when.
